## TL;DR
This project is an AI-driven sustainable architectural planning platform. It automates the process of analyzing a geospatial plot of land (via satellite and environmental data), determining "buildability" (flood risk, legal zoning), and procedurally generating optimized, eco-friendly floor plans using a Genetic Algorithm (GA) and an iterative correction loop.

## Design Overview
*   **Hybrid AI Pipeline**: Combines traditional ML (MLPs for flood/buildability), Computer Vision (satellite segmentation for boundaries/trees), and Metaheuristics (Genetic Algorithms for spatial layout).
*   **Asynchronous Geospatial Orchestration**: Uses Python's `asyncio` and `httpx` to concurrently fetch heavy environmental data (solar, wind, rainfall) from multiple external APIs to minimize latency.
*   **State-Driven 3D Frontend**: A Next.js application utilizing `react-three-fiber` and `Zustand` to map complex backend geometric data into an interactive 3D environment with real-time lighting and environmental simulations.
*   **Corrective Feedback Loop**: Instead of a single-pass generation, the system uses an `eco_validator` to audit plans against sustainability criteria, feeding errors back into a `correction_loop` to mutate and refine the architecture.

## Code Walkthrough

### Backend: The Intelligence Layer
#### `backend\ai\floorplan\` — [The Core Engine]
*   **`ga_engine.py` & `genetic_optimizer.py`**: Implements the NSGA-III (Non-dominated Sorted Genetic Algorithm) to balance competing objectives (e.g., maximizing sunlight vs. minimizing material waste).
*   **`correction_loop.py` & `correction_strategies.py`**: These files implement a "critic-actor" pattern. The validator identifies failures (e.g., "room too small"), and the strategy files define how to mutate the "chromosome" (the floorplan layout) to fix specifically that error.
*   **`eco_validator.py`**: The system's "conscience." It calculates solar azimuths and wind bearings to score the plan's environmental efficiency.

#### `backend\services\` & `backend\ai\buildability\flood\` — [Environmental Analysis]
*   **`analysis_pipeline.py`**: The orchestrator. It sequences the segmentation $\rightarrow$ tree detection $\rightarrow$ environmental fetch $\rightarrow$ risk modeling.
*   **`real_env_data.py`**: A critical integration layer that translates GPS coordinates into environmental metrics (NDVI, slope, soil type).
*   **`flood_model.py` & `buildability_model.py`**: Use pickled ML models to provide binary/probabilistic classifications of whether a plot is viable for construction.

## Concepts & Patterns

**Genetic Algorithms (GA)**
- **What**: An optimization technique based on natural selection (mutation, crossover, fitness).
- **Why used here**: Architectural layout is a "combinatorial explosion" problem. There are too many ways to arrange rooms to check every possibility; GA finds "good enough" solutions quickly.
- **Trade-offs**: You gain global optimization; you lose guaranteed convergence to the *absolute* best solution.
- **When to use**: When the search space is massive and the "fitness" (quality) is easy to measure but hard to calculate mathematically.

**Asyncio / Concurrent I/O**
- **What**: Non-blocking event loop for handling I/O-bound tasks.
- **Why used here**: Fetching satellite tiles and environmental data from 5+ different APIs would take 10+ seconds if done sequentially; `asyncio.gather` drops this to the time of the slowest single request.
- **Trade-offs**: Code complexity increases (async/await keywords); potential for overloading external APIs (rate limiting).
- **When to use**: High-latency network integrations.

**Correction Loop (Feedback Pattern)**
- **What**: A cycle where an output is validated, errors are identified, and the input is adjusted.
- **Why used here**: GA is great at general shapes but bad at specific "rules" (e.g., "doors must not face walls"). The correction loop fixes these "edge cases" without ruining the GA's global optimization.
- **Trade-offs**: Higher computational cost per generation.
- **When to use**: When you have a mix of "fuzzy" goals (eco-friendliness) and "hard" constraints (building codes).
